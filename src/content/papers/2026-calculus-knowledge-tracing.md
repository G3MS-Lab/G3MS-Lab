High school Calculus education faces significant challenges due to heavy teacher workloads, which often limit the ability to track student mastery at an individual level. Standard assessment methods frequently lack qualitative feedback and do not adequately support different learning paces. This study addresses these limitations by developing an intelligent learning platform for introductory Calculus based on advanced Knowledge Tracing (KT) models. A comprehensive comparative analysis was conducted across three categories of KT approaches: probabilistic Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) variants, logistic models (PFA), and Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) architectures, including LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and GRU. The evaluation used augmented datasets derived from empirical student interaction data. Model performance was assessed using predictive accuracy (AUC), data efficiency, and computational scalability. The results show that traditional models provide strong interpretability and low computational cost. However, the DKT-GRU model consistently outperforms other approaches, achieving a peak Test AUC of approximately 0.68-0.7. It effectively captures non-linear dependencies and sequential learning patterns in Calculus. In addition, the integration of response latency and diagonal clustering enables the system to distinguish true mastery from unstable behaviors such as lucky guessing and careless slips. These findings identify DKT-GRU as the most effective engine for supporting real-time personalized learning analytics and adaptive testing. The proposed platform provides a scalable solution for improving instructional decision-making and enhancing individualized learning in mathematics education.
