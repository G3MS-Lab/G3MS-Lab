To address the challenge of low audience engagement in live streaming, this research presents the
development of an interactive horror game tailored for streamers, aiming to transform passive viewership
into active participation. The game allows viewers to influence gameplay in real-time by triggering in-game
events through Streamlabs donation interactions, thereby creating a more immersive and engaging experience.
Built using Godot 4 for game development, Node.js and WebSocket for real-time backend communication, and React.js for the frontend interface, the system enables seamless low-latency interaction between
viewers and the game. The design is inspired by the success of NPC-style streams and grounded in game
design frameworks such as MDA (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics) and the Eight Kinds of Fun, ensuring
both emotional resonance and gameplay variety.
What sets this work apart is the integration of gamified donation mechanics in a horror-survival setting,
where viewer actions directly sabotage or assist the streamer. Additionally, survey data from both streamers
and audiences informed the design choices, ensuring relevance and appeal to the target demographic.
As a result, the project not only enhances streamer-viewer interactivity but also introduces a novel
”Stream-to-Earn” model that creates new monetization opportunities for content creators through gameplaydriven donation systems.
